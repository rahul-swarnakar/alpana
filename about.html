Our name

Why "Alpona"

An alpona is the traditional Bengali floor art drawn in rice paste at the threshold of a home — for a festival, a wedding, a harvest. It is never copied wholesale from one doorstep to the next; it is drawn fresh, by hand, to suit the space in front of it.

We took the name because it describes how we try to work: livelihood promotion, awareness, and need assessment are not three separate departments, but three strokes of the same pattern, drawn together, household by household.

What guides us

Mission & approach

Listen first

Every programme begins with a household-level need assessment, not a template brought from outside.

Work with existing skill

Livelihood support builds on crafts and trades a family already practises, rather than replacing them.

Stay local

Rooted in one block — Bolpur — so relationships with families and local institutions run deep rather than wide.

Since 2013–14

Milestones

  • 2013–14
    ALPANA registered as a society (Reg. No. S/2L/9874), based in Bhubandanga, Bolpur.
  • Early years
    First need-assessment surveys carried out across neighbouring villages to identify where support was most wanted.
  • Since then
    Livelihood and awareness activities built up around the skills and gaps those surveys surfaced.
  • Ongoing
    Continuing work from our Bolpur office, near Lodge More.
